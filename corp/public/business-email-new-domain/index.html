A new domain is a brand promise. The first email a customer gets from it should look like it came from a company that has its act together: your name, your domain, signed and verified.

Two kinds of "business email"

  • Your inbox. you@yourdomain for one-to-one mail. Your domain registrar or a mailbox host covers this.
  • Your list. Launch notes, newsletters and product updates sent to many people at once. This is where MailCheap fits.

Setting up the domain

  1. Buy the domain and point it at your site. If the site isn't up yet, see startup website hosting.
  2. Add the DNS records your email tools give you (SPF, DKIM and DMARC). MailCheap's Domains page lists the ones it needs.
  3. Send a test to yourself before the first real campaign.

Questions

What is the cheapest way to send email from a new domain?

For newsletters, launch notes and updates, MailCheap's plans start at $19 a month (Starter, up to 10,000 subscribers), and every plan includes your own domain (pricing page, checked November 2, 2026).

Is MailCheap a mailbox for my day-to-day inbox?

No. MailCheap is for sending newsletters and updates to a list. For a personal inbox like you@yourdomain, use a mailbox host or the one your domain registrar offers.

Do I need my own domain to start on MailCheap?

No. MailCheap's FAQ says you can start on a free address it provides and add your own domain any time; on your own domain, its Domains page gives you the DNS records to add.

Is there a free trial?

MailCheap's pricing FAQ says no: plans start at $19 a month, and you can cancel anytime.
